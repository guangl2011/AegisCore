## 1. Technology Innovation

Commercial outdoor autonomous service robots — turf-care mowers, municipal maintenance machines, inspection platforms — are locked to proprietary OEM controllers: closed boards, opaque safety circuits, and unverified command links. Mixed-brand fleets run segregated maintenance pipelines, and a foreign-manufactured controller cannot be audited by the municipalities that deploy it. Swapping foreign controllers for domestic copies is manufacturing, not science. The open research question is whether one edge architecture can provably enforce safety across heterogeneous, untrusted actuators while translating a single command language to many chassis.

No shipping product answers it. Fleet-orchestration platforms (InOrbit, Formant) monitor and dispatch but never touch low-level control; ROS-Industrial explicitly does not replace OEM controllers; universal controller hardware (SEER's SRC series) targets indoor AMR builders. A retrofit deterministic safety supervisor with a zero-trust gateway for outdoor robots does not exist today.

The scientific lineage exists and AegisCore unites it: reference governors enforce state and control constraints pointwise in time; CBF QP filters compute minimum-norm safe commands in real time; Safe-ROS separates intelligence from an independent safety system holding e-stop authority; safety-MCU patterns enforce fixed-period loops with watchdogs serviced only after a valid cycle. AegisCore unites this lineage in one verified edge stack:

1. **Deterministic Safety-Bounded Command Governor.** The sole writer of actuator commands: a runtime supervisor enforcing kinetic-energy envelopes, proximity-based e-stop precedence, command-age validation, and watchdog invariants independent of upstream commands or compromised vendor firmware. Every cycle appends a decision record — command, margins, mode — to the safety log: 100% deterministic safety logging by construction.

2. **Semantic Capability Abstraction Layer (SCAL).** A validated state-action mapping translating task primitives (MoveTo, FollowPath, SetBlade, Dock) into chassis-specific command streams across differential, Ackermann-steer, and hybrid multi-motor kinematics, with per-command latency instrumentation and property-based envelope conformance.

3. **Cyber-Resilient Zero-Trust Gateway.** Certificate-based mutual authentication, per-session command leases, and nonce-based replay rejection assert a valid envelope before any command reaches translation; on hostile communication loss or spoofing, a deterministic six-state failover ladder descends to controlled stop and never defaults to nominal. It admits only hash-pinned model artifacts before any perception service is exposed.

4. **Domestic Hardware-Assurance Topology.** A control-board topology for verifiable U.S. surface-mount assembly, with hardware root of trust and auditable microcode, aligned with NDAA Section 889 and federal covered-list procurement rules. Perception acceleration targets domestic Intel edge silicon — OpenVINO inference on Core Ultra-class NPUs — advisory-only and off the deterministic control path.

In simulation the stack runs end to end: translation p50 66.5 µs and p99 184 µs against the 5 ms aim over a 100-trajectory, three-chassis benchmark; maximum fail-safe transition 30 ms against the 50 ms aim across an eight-fault-class matrix; zero kinematic violations; all 1,998,144 governor decisions logged; and an advisory experiment measuring the cost of perception-driven safety signals.

## 2. Technical Objectives and Challenges

Each aim is a research question with a named experiment. The CI-run bench harness — a fixed-seed 100-trajectory corpus and an 8-fault × 3-chassis fault matrix — reports measurements; hardware-dependent evidence stays a Phase I target with its experiment named.

**Aim 1 — Semantic translation.** *Research question:* can one semantic abstraction translate task primitives across three kinematic models in under 5 ms per command, with zero kinematic violations? *Experiment:* the benchmark measures per-command translation latency over the corpus and asserts actuator-limit conformance via property tests. *Measured:* p50 66.5 µs, p99 184 µs, max 492 µs over 1,161 commands — roughly 27× under the bound — with zero kinematic violations. Phase I reproduces it on live OEM adapters, where risk shifts to proprietary drivers.

**Aim 2 — Deterministic fail-safe governor.** *Research question:* does a six-state failover governor guarantee a defined safe state within 50 ms under arbitrary fault injection, with complete decision logging? *Experiment:* an 8-fault × 3-chassis matrix — spoofed command, stale heartbeat, sensor loss, network loss, watchdog miss, braking loss, proximity e-stop, unmodeled fault — asserting transition latency, safe-state termination, and log completeness. *Measured:* max transition 30 ms across all 24 scenarios; every scenario in a defined safe state; 1,998,144 of 1,998,144 decisions logged (100%); unmodeled faults default to controlled stop, never nominal. These are algorithmic latencies — Python cannot prove WCET. Phase I moves enforcement onto a safety MCU, where the fixed-period loop and hardware watchdog make the bound a hardware contract with measured jitter.

**Aim 3 — Adapter complexity and field trials.** *Research question:* does one validated profile per chassis cut adapter configuration effort by ≥30% versus per-OEM integration, with zero safety compromise under loss-of-network? *Experiment:* count the configuration surface per profile against a per-OEM baseline, then measure operator effort in HIL setups and outdoor trials on three platforms. Simulation cannot produce this; the MVP ships the scaffold — configuration-surface counting and the network-loss fault class — and ≥30% remains a Phase I target.

**Aim 4 — Perception-driven advisory safety.** *Research question:* can perception-driven advisory signals, accelerated on Intel edge hardware, narrow safety margins fast enough to be operationally useful without ever touching the command path? *Experiment:* the harness re-runs the corpus and fault matrix with an advisory provider feeding the governor's margin inputs, measuring consumption latency, descent coverage, and stale-constraint rejection. *Measured (stub providers; CI runs no inference):* consumption adds p99 13.8 µs per governor cycle; advisory-driven descents fired at 160 per 10,000 cycles; stale constraints were rejected. Real NPU inference latency is a named Phase I measurement. Advisory output can narrow a margin but never write a command.

**Key technical challenges.** *Kinematic mismatch:* command authority across machines with differing torque curves and stopping inertias — profile-declared limits plus governor envelope projection. *Low-latency determinism:* invariant checks on low-power microcontrollers without jitter — the safety-MCU partition. *Fault isolation:* trapping faults inside proprietary drivers without cascading bus faults — bounded-deceleration fallbacks and the braking-loss e-stop path.

## 3. Market Opportunity

The commercial robotic mower market — robotic turf-care equipment for contractors, campuses, and municipal grounds — is projected to grow from $2.1–2.8B in 2025 to about $3.4B by 2030, a 10–14% CAGR (Ken Research 2025; DataIntelo 2025; Mordor Intelligence 2026). Policy converts growth into procurement: in July 2026 the FCC added foreign-produced advanced robotic devices to its Covered List, citing data exfiltration, remote disruption, and possible remote commandeering; an October 2026 White House proclamation flags imported-robotics cyber risk; CISA and FBI guidance requires bill-of-materials transparency, U.S.-controlled updates, and independent testing. Municipalities must now justify foreign-connected equipment; domestic, verifiable, NDAA-compliant edge control — on a supply chain including the Intel-based edge compute it targets for perception — is a procurement necessity, not a preference.

The pain is pre-existing: mixed-brand fleets run segregated maintenance pipelines on disparate proprietary controllers; operators report single-source OEM replacement boards with 8–12 week lead times and liability exposure with no independent incident record.

Beachhead: domestic landscape contractors and autonomous-mower fleet operators running 20–150 units, starting with pilot operators already supplying chassis, field logs, and trial sites. Spotlize1 monetizes three ways: sales of the U.S.-assembled, NDAA-compliant controller/gateway hardware module; recurring per-device annual software licenses for the supervisor and telemetry; and hardware-migration certification packages for integrators. After Phase I the same controller, profiles, and safety evidence extend to snow removal, airport inspection, and industrial sweepers.

Industry partners: commercial landscaping pilot operators provide real mower chassis, operational field logs, and facilities for controlled outdoor trials. These pilot operators are the named initial customers for the Phase I beachhead: their fleets are the first deployments of the controller, and their trial results form the adapter-complexity evidence for Aim 3.

Capability-gap plan: Phase I adds embedded firmware engineering for safety-MCU integration and safety-certification support through contractors or hires, with the academic subaward covering formal verification review — the two capabilities the current team does not yet hold in depth.
